# Korean Train Punctuality website

This public repository is [Bttlbmb/korean-train-punctuality](https://github.com/Bttlbmb/korean-train-punctuality). It holds the static site: a front page (`index.html`), one page for each train (`app.html?train=<id>`), one page for each of the past 30 days (`app.html?day=<date>`), and one static page for each complete calendar month (`<year>-<month>/`, such as `2026-10/`). It uses the Porcelain palette in `tokens.css`, SVG and canvas drawing, and plain JavaScript modules, with no build step or third-party scripts.

## Preview

```sh
python3 scripts/preview.py
```

Open <http://127.0.0.1:8765/> for the front page and, for example, <http://127.0.0.1:8765/app.html?train=456-ktx> for a train page. The server binds only to the local machine.

`data/` holds the export that the backend’s `exporter/export_site_data.py` writes. The site reads `meta.json`, `days.json`, `windows/yesterday.json`, `windows/month.json`, `stations/index.json`, `trains/<id>.json`, and `days/<date>.json`. `meta.json` records the complete-date range and any date still collecting. To refresh the site, run the export described in the backend’s `BUILD_NOTES.md`, which writes here by default when the two repositories sit side by side.

## Definitions and decisions

- The site draws figures from the exporter’s files; the backend’s `BUILD_NOTES.md` owns the metric definitions.
- Day by day preserves calendar gaps and distinguishes dates not collected from unavailable or missing records. Chart values are also exposed in screen-reader tables; marks support keyboard focus and touch tooltips.
- Guides describe the current state, and git history is the archive; the owner decided this on 8 October 2026. Completed work gets no dated report or “before” copy; the commit message records the change and its checks.
- Repository documentation follows [the writing guide](docs/writing.md): American spelling with day-first dates such as 8 October 2026, matching the existing documents and the site’s own date format. It applies to text written or edited from 8 October 2026; existing passages are not rewritten for style alone. Each repository keeps its own copy of the guide; the copies differ only where they name that repository’s documents, so apply a change to both.
- The owner split the project into the `backend` and `website` repositories on 23 October 2026, without carrying over the earlier git history.
- The owner named the public site Korean Train Punctuality, placed it at `Bttlbmb/korean-train-punctuality`, and kept its scraper and exporter in the private `Bttlbmb/korean-train-punctuality-generator` repository.

## Interface

The data the site reads is listed in the backend’s `BUILD_NOTES.md` under Published files, and that guide owns the metric definitions.

- **Start page:** `index.html`, with `js/start.js`, is the front page: a single page with no navigation, whose Yesterday dots lead to the train pages. It has one row per period, Yesterday and the past 30 days. Each row has a 64-pixel share of stops within 5 minutes, one sentence, and support lines (the typical delay when late, and for the past 30 days the best and worst days), and a visual of the same height. Yesterday draws one dot per train in order of departure, in the late color when the train had at least one reported stop more than 5 minutes late (a train with no countable stops is grey). Hover and arrow keys name the train. A click, or Enter on a selected dot, opens its page; on touch screens the first tap selects a dot, and a second tap on it opens the page. The bars of the past 30 days open their day page in the same way, except that a day not collected is not a link. A screen-reader table lists the trains with a late stop. The past 30 days draws one bar per calendar day on a true 0–100% axis, and a short outlined stub marks a day not collected. Hover, touch, and arrow keys on the bars show that day’s share and late stops. When the window holds fewer than 30 complete days, the sentence says “the N days collected so far.” The page says “yesterday” and “past 30 days” only when the newest complete date is yesterday in Korea time. Otherwise the labels name the dates: the Yesterday row shows the weekday and date, and the 30-day row shows the date range and says “the 30 days to 24 Oct.” The page reads `meta.json`, `days.json`, and the Yesterday and Past month window files.
- **Train page:** `app.html?train=<id>`, with `js/app.js` and `js/train.js`, has no navigation. Only the site name heads the page, and it links to the front page. A request without a well-formed train id goes to the front page; an id with no train file shows an empty state. The page always covers the past month, so it has no window tabs and loads no window file.
  - **Heading:** a back link to the front page, the title, and a meta line (scheduled times, stops, and the date range), then three figures in one row: Stops within 5 min, On time at the final stop, and Typical delay when late. A small map of South Korea sits to the right, centered on the title, meta line, and figures: a grey landmass with the route in ink, drawn from `latitude` and `longitude` in `stations/index.json` and the outline in `js/korea.js`. It is decoration, so screen readers skip it, and it is left out unless every stop has coordinates.
  - **Stop list:** the card title is “Delay at every stop in the past 30 days.” There is a row for each stop, with its scheduled time at the left: the arrival, or at the origin the departure, marked “departs”. The origin has no arrival delay, so its row has no dots. A time after midnight carries a small +1. Each row shows one dot per recorded day at its delay in whole minutes, green within 5 minutes and orange later, with a dashed line between 5 and 6 minutes. A small “5 min” label above the first row names that line; the list has no minute scale, because the card caption says the unit is minutes. Stop names are plain text. Each row shows its share of days within 5 minutes at the right, or a dash when no arrival was reported; the tooltip gives the full count. A small “within 5 min” label in the origin row, which has no share, names that column. On phones the “departs” mark is hidden and the time column narrows.
  - **Day by day:** one cell per calendar day, filling the card’s width, colored by the delay at the final stop. A mark shows a timetable change. The strip is a single tab stop: the arrow keys step through the days and show each day’s tooltip, and Escape clears it. A screen-reader table lists every day.
- **Day page:** `app.html?day=<date>`, with `js/app.js` and `js/day.js`, has the same brand-only header as the train page. A request with a malformed or impossible date goes to the front page; a date with no `days/<date>.json` shows an empty state and asks not to be indexed.
  - **Heading:** a back button to the front page, the date as the title, a meta line (trains and stops), and three figures in one row: stops within 5 min, trains with a late stop, and typical delay when late. A train counts when it has a countable arrival.
  - **How late stops were:** one bar across the card, split by delay in the delay colors: within 5 min, 6–10, 11–20, 21–30, and over 30 minutes, sized by stops, so its green share is the heading's figure. Labels with the share sit under the segments wide enough for one (8% or more); under 760 pixels a legend with every bucket replaces them. Hover names a segment and its stop count, and a screen-reader table lists every bucket.
  - **Biggest delays:** up to 10 trains with the largest delay at any stop (more than 5 minutes only). Each row has the train (a link to its page), route with the scheduled departure and arrival time beside the first and last station (left out for a day file without them), the stop where the delay was largest, the scheduled arrival there, the reported arrival, and the delay. Korail reports a delay and no observed arrival time, so the reported arrival is the scheduled arrival plus the reported delay, and a time past midnight carries a small +1. Under 760 pixels the route and stop columns are hidden. A day with no late stop says so instead of showing a table.
- **Search:** every train page sets its own title (“KTX 456 Mokpo → Seoul · Korean Train Punctuality”), description (the share of stops within 5 minutes), and canonical link, from `pageInfo` in `js/train.js`. A train with no run in the past month gets `noindex`, because its page is empty. A day page sets its own title and description from `pageInfo` in `js/day.js` and a canonical link, and is not listed in `sitemap.xml`. A month page sets its own title, description, and canonical link in its markup, which the exporter writes. `sitemap.xml` at the site root lists the front page, every train page with a run in the past month, and every month page; the exporter writes it, and the nightly job publishes it with `data/`. A project site cannot serve `robots.txt` (search engines read only the host root), so submit `https://bttlbmb.github.io/korean-train-punctuality/sitemap.xml` once in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
- **Colors:** `tokens.css` holds the Porcelain palette: nearly neutral greys, an ink-navy accent, and a muted terracotta delay ramp. Green means on time, the warm ramp means late, grey is neutral, and blue marks focus. An arrival within 5 minutes counts as on time, so delay scales color it green (`--ontime` for bars and cells, the darker `--ontime-mark` for dots), and `--d1` appears only in share scales. The accent marks links, hover, and focus; headline figures use `--ink`. `theme-color` repeats the canvas value.
- **Legends:** every color scale has a legend that names what it measures and labels each step.

## Checks

- Run `node scripts/check_trains.mjs` after changing the train page or refreshing `data/`. It renders every train page, and every day page when `data/days/` exists, and fails on an error, text such as “undefined” or “NaN”, a final stop whose dots do not match the heading’s reported count, a row whose share does not match its dots, or a missing, overlong, or (for an empty page) present title and description. For a day page it also fails when the delay buckets do not add up to the stops, or the stops or share differ from `days.json`. It needs Node 18 or newer and is not part of the site.
- In the local preview, check the front page, a train page, and a day page at 360, 768, 1,280, and 1,440 pixels: the dot click-through, Back, the empty, error, and missing-data states, and that no request fails. Wide content scrolls inside its own container, never the page.
